Screen. Record. Preserve the baseline.

Use this guide to document the starting condition of a newly purchased MP5-pattern host before its troubleshooting history begins. Follow the retailer’s handling rules and the manufacturer’s instructions for your exact model. A counter inspection is a preliminary screen, not a complete safety or reliability certification.

Check bolt gap before accepting the firearm

Bolt gap is an important inspection measurement on an MP5-pattern firearm. When the retailer permits it, have this checked and documented before completing acceptance.

Use the manufacturer’s procedure and specification for the exact model. Do not substitute a specification from another brand or assume that a general internet recommendation applies.

For an inspection with a feeler gauge:

  1. Obtain the retailer’s permission. Keep the muzzle pointed in the designated safe direction and remove all ammunition from the inspection area.
  2. Remove the magazine and verify that the chamber and magazine well are empty.
  3. Establish the bolt and hammer condition required by the manufacturer’s measurement procedure. An incorrectly prepared firearm can produce a misleading reading.
  4. Using a clean, undamaged feeler gauge, check the clearance between the bolt head and bolt carrier at the manufacturer-designated measurement location. Use light pressure; do not force a blade into the gap or use it to move the components.
  5. Record on the Acceptance Worksheet the blade thickness that fits with slight drag and the next thicker blade that does not. Include the units.
  6. Compare the result with the manufacturer’s specification. Have an uncertain or borderline result confirmed by the dealer’s gunsmith or the manufacturer before treating it as a pass.

Our acceptance recommendation: Do not accept a new firearm with a confirmed out-of-spec bolt gap on the assumption that it can be corrected later. Pause acceptance and resolve the finding with the retailer. An abnormal starting measurement may indicate an assembly, dimensional, or component issue that warrants evaluation.

An owner’s manual may not include a bolt-gap procedure or a new-firearm acceptance limit. Where the applicable guidance is absent, obtain model-specific clarification from the manufacturer or importer before assigning a pass or fail.

If the retailer does not permit the check

Some retailers require a trigger lock to remain installed or prohibit the handling necessary to complete this inspection. Respect those restrictions. Record “Not checked: retailer restriction”, rather than marking the firearm as passing.

If a subsequent inspection identifies an out-of-spec condition, stop use and promptly contact the manufacturer or importer’s warranty support. Preserve the original configuration, receipt, serial number, and inspection findings. Obtain their instructions before authorizing changes.

For AP5 owners, Century Arms directs warranty inquiries through its contact page or support@centuryarms.com. See its official AP5 FAQ.

Check magazine seating and retention

Perform this check only with the FFL’s permission, using an unloaded firearm and an empty, compatible magazine. Keep ammunition away from the inspection area, maintain a safe muzzle direction, and keep your finger outside the trigger guard.

  1. Verify that the chamber and magazine are empty.
  2. Pull the charging handle fully rearward and rotate it upward into its locking notch.
  3. Insert the empty magazine firmly until the magazine catch engages.
  4. Before releasing the bolt, give the magazine a gentle downward pull without touching either magazine release. It should remain secured.
  5. With the magazine seated, release the charging handle from its notch and allow the bolt to travel forward under spring pressure, following the manufacturer’s instructions. Do not guide it forward. The familiar “HK slap” is a way of releasing the handle, not a requirement to strike it forcefully.
  6. Check retention again. Some movement can be normal, but the magazine should not disengage during this check unless the release button or paddle is operated.
  7. Remove the magazine, verify the firearm is clear, and repeat with each supplied magazine the retailer permits you to inspect.

A magazine that will not latch, releases without operating the catch, or requires excessive force warrants evaluation before acceptance. Record on the Acceptance Worksheet which magazine exhibited the problem. An empty-magazine retention check does not establish feeding reliability.

If the magazine is securely retained but feels looser than you prefer, record that observation separately. Turkwerk can evaluate the fit during its service process and determine whether an appropriate adjustment is available. Not every amount of movement is a defect, and not every concern requires modification.

If the retailer prohibits this check, mark “Not checked: retailer restriction.” on the Acceptance Worksheet.

Record the starting condition

Retain the exact model and serial number, purchase date, retailer, configuration as received, inspection findings, and any restrictions that prevented a check. Identify each magazine tested. Keep the receipt and supporting photographs with the record.

Use the companion Acceptance Worksheet

The New Host Acceptance Guide explains what each screening item means and when a check should stop. The companion New Host Acceptance Worksheet is the one-page field record.

Use the worksheet to record the checks you were permitted to perform, any retailer restrictions, the bolt-gap result and specification source when measured, magazine-retention observations, the host's as-received configuration, and the final acceptance decision.

“Not checked” is a valid result. Do not infer a pass from a blank field and do not perform a check that the retailer prohibits. If you proceed to Turkwerk Initial Host Validation, retain the completed worksheet with the host's purchase records and provide the relevant observations during intake.

Professional validation

Purchase the firearm through your preferred dealer. Then put the host through Turkwerk Initial Host Validation.

The service documents the starting condition, evaluates the host against defined acceptance criteria, and completes the agreed testing. A successfully validated host is returned with its tested configuration, results, and limitations documented. A host that cannot meet the criteria receives a documented hold, manufacturer referral, or decline instead of a passing report.
